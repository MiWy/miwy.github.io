General info

Having psychological or other difficulties? (click)

If you’re feeling overwhelmed by your studies and facing challenges beyond your resources and abilities, you can reach out to the psychological consultant for study-related difficulties .

If you’re going through a difficult time or need support for any reason, don’t hesitate or feel ashamed to seek psychological help . Additionally, our faculty members offer free psychological support in the form of phone consultation for students.

If you’re facing technological or health-related difficulties that prevent or significantly hinder your studies, please contact me so we can work out a plan for your participation.

This elective course is dedicated to developing your ability to analyze and address ethical challenges related to neuroscience, AI, and broader technological advancements.

Key details:

  • We meet on Thursdays at 1:15PM.
  • Each session explores a different ethical challenge related to AI, neuroethics, and technology.
  • Office hours, i.e., time: Wednesdays 8:30–9:30AM (Room 65AB). Email: michal.wyrwa [at] amu.edu.pl.
  • Discussions on morally sensitive topics require a safe environment—let’s work together to create an inclusive and constructive space.
  • Prior experience with philosophy, neuroscience, or AI is not required (but always nice to have:)). The focus is on practical ethical reasoning, not technical expertise or history of philosophy.
  • Readings and handouts are available here.
  • Note that readings are available for each session. They are listed in the discussion questions. Some of them are marked with a ‘★’—these are particularly important for your future self preparing for the test. By early December, you will receive a detailed list of exam questions and key topics.
  • The readings are there for your reference: ★-marked ones are required for the test, but you don’t have to read them for our meetings. For discussion purposes, the short material listed under “To do” is enough.

A tour of the topics and cases we’ll discuss; overview of the course structure and some foundations in ethics.

(22.10) Neuroscience & responsibility

Drugs that switch off cravings: what do they change about how we blame people for addiction and obesity?

  • Listen to one podcast: TED Radio Hour or Arnold Ventures.

(29.10) Neuroenhancements

Ethical and practical considerations of using neuroenhancing substances and technologies.

  • Find two real-life examples of neuroenhancement: one with a positive and one with a negative effect (according to you).
  • ★ Forlini, C., Hall, W., Maxwell, B., Outram, S. M., Reiner, P. B., Repantis, D., … & Racine, E. (2013). Navigating the enhancement landscape: Ethical issues in research on cognitive enhancers for healthy individuals. EMBO Reports, 14(2), 123–128.

Course Format & Assessment

Assessment structure:

  1. Test (60pt)
    • Questions on the ★ readings.
  2. Being active in class (30pt)
    • Active participation in discussions, debates, and group work.
  3. Presentation or oral exam (optional, 10pt)
    • Without it, you can get 4.5 max. For a 5.0, you need one of the two.
    • Presentation: up to 25 minutes, individually, deepening the topic of one session. One presentation per session; the sessions with a slot are marked in the schedule above.
    • Oral exam: on 14.01, a short discussion of an ethical case we haven’t covered in class.

In total, you can get 100pt:

  • from 93% – 5.0 (≥93pt)
  • from 85% to 92% – 4.5 (≥85pt)
  • from 76% to 84% – 4.0 (≥76pt)
  • from 68% to 75% – 3.5 (≥68pt)
  • from 60% to 67% – 3.0 (≥60pt)
  • less than 60% – 2.0
